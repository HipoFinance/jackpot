# Hipo Jackpot v2

**Status:** approved

Supersedes [`2026-10-27-jackpot-v1.md`](2026-10-27-jackpot-v1.md). v1 was never deployed, so v2 changes the
storage layout and the messages freely. A rule of v1 that this document does not change still holds, and
its reasons stay in v1.

## Goal

Make the Jackpot simple to hold and cheap to run: hGRAM deposits only, one non-transferable jGRAM wallet per
player, draws that run on a fixed clock from one message, and an entry price in place of any waiting period.
Unused gas goes back to the player, and draw gas comes out of the yield.

## In plain terms

Players stake on Hipo first, then move their hGRAM into the Jackpot. The Jackpot records how much GRAM that
hGRAM was worth. This is the player's principal, and it never changes unless they add or withdraw. The player
sees it as a token called jGRAM in their wallet app: 1 jGRAM is 1 GRAM of principal. jGRAM cannot be sent to
anyone. Each player has one jGRAM wallet however many times they deposit.

The hGRAM keeps growing in value. The growth above what the Jackpot owes the players is the pot. On a fixed
clock, once per validation round (about 18 hours), three prizes are drawn from the pot, with chances in
proportion to principal. A share goes to the HPO burn as before. Winners receive hGRAM automatically.

**No waiting.** A deposit can win the very next draw. To stop someone jumping in just before a draw and
taking a share of a pot they did not help grow, a deposit pays an entry price: a small part of it goes into
the pot, in proportion to how large the pot is now. If the pot is 0.03% of all principal, a 1,000 GRAM deposit
books about 999.7 GRAM of principal. Right after a draw the pot is about empty, so the price is about zero. The
price is exactly what the deposit's share of the current pot is worth, so buying in late gains nothing on
average. Withdrawals pay no price, and a player who leaves gives up their chance at the pot. If the pot is
ever unusually large, a deposit that would pay over 1% is sent back unless the app asked for it.

**How a draw runs.** The clock ticks at fixed times. Nothing is sent at the tick; the first message of any
kind after it notices, and fixes who is in the draw exactly as things stood at the tick. A minute later drand,
a public random beacon, publishes a number. Anyone can bring that number to the Jackpot, which checks its
signature, pays the sender back with a small reward, and then does everything else by itself: it fixes the
pot, finds the three winners and pays them. Hipo will run two small services that do this, but anyone can.

**During a draw.** Withdrawals keep paying at once and deposits keep working. The running count the draw
searches stays as it was at the tick, so that nobody who has seen the random number can move a prize. A player
who withdraws during a draw simply loses their chance in it. A deposit made during a draw joins a later one.
Until the pot is set aside, such a deposit pays no entry price, since that pot is not for it.

**If nobody brings the number** by half an hour before the next tick, because drand is down or no one sent it,
that draw waits for the next tick's number. Nothing is lost, and the pot grows. Deposits made meanwhile stay out
until the waiting draw has been paid, and during that time their yield goes to the pot. Withdrawals never stop.

**Gas.** Deposits and withdrawals are paid by the user, and whatever a step does not use comes back. Draws are
paid from the yield: the Jackpot keeps some GRAM and now and then unstakes a little of the pot through Hipo.

**Leaving.** When a player withdraws everything, their jGRAM wallet closes and returns its small GRAM reserve.
A later deposit opens it again.

**What stays the same.** Principal is paid back in hGRAM at Hipo's rate. The drand check, the prize split, the
burn, the governor's powers and all the rules about recorded payouts stay. Hipo's contracts do not change.

**Trade-offs and risks.** A new contract with many moving parts holds user funds, and the audits start over
for the changed parts. If no one submits the random number while the prizes are up, a player could submit only
when they win, so a skipped draw is treated as an alarm. A player's jGRAM wallet that runs out of GRAM for
storage after many years stops until someone restores it. Deposits made during a long drand outage earn no
chance until it ends.

**Decisions needed:** none. The two open questions are decided; see Open questions.

## Definitions

**Concepts**

- **Principal**: the GRAM a player is owed back, fixed when a deposit is booked.
- **Quote**: the pair (`total_coins`, `total_tokens`) the treasury sends in reply to `provide_current_quote`: its
  rate, GRAM per hGRAM. The Jackpot stores the latest.
- **Slot**: one of the three prizes of a draw (0 = 50%, 1 = 30%, 2 = 20%). **Attempt**: one position tried for a slot.
- **Carried**: prizes gone back into `F` since the last draw that fixed a pot. They were already counted for the burn.
- **Burner**: Hipo's HPO burner contract, which swaps the hGRAM it receives into HPO and burns it.
- **drand quicknet**: a public randomness network with a round every 3 s, checked on chain as in v1.
- **Booking**: the moment a deposit becomes principal, when the Jackpot handles the treasury's quote reply.
- **jGRAM**: the Jackpot's jetton. Master: the Jackpot. Balance: the player's principal, in nano-GRAM.
- **jGRAM wallet**: the contract at the address derived from (Jackpot, owner). It stores the owner, the
  balance, the position and `pending`. Below, "wallet" alone means this one; the other is "hGRAM wallet".
- **Position**: `b`, a number below 16,777,216 that places a wallet in the tree. One wallet has at most one.
  A wallet that closes loses its position; the number is never used again.
- **Opening**: a wallet with no position and `pending` > 0: its first booking is under way.
- **Ticket**: one nano-GRAM of principal counted in the tree. Tickets line up by `b`.
- **Node**: as in v1: 64 children, 4 levels, addressed by (Jackpot, level, index). A level-1 node's children are
  positions and it also stores each position's owner.
- **Snapshot**: the tree as it stood at a boundary, kept in each node's `committed` array. It is what a draw
  searches.
- **Tag**: a counter raised at each new snapshot. It travels on every tree message. A node that sees a later
  tag folds its held changes into `committed` first.
- **Held changes**: the `added` and `removed` arrays of a node: tickets booked and tickets withdrawn since
  the snapshot.
- **Boundary**: a time `T_n` of the schedule. **Period**: the time between two boundaries.
- **Tick**: the check that runs first on every state-changing message and acts on any boundary that has passed.
- **Open draw**: a draw from its boundary until it ends. Its states are *waiting* (for randomness), *quoting*
  (randomness checked, quote asked), *resolving* (prizes fixed, searches running). With no open draw the state
  is *none*.
- **Skip**: a draw still waiting or quoting at the next boundary. It stays open and waits for the new
  boundary's drand round.
- **Search**: the walk of one attempt down the tree. No wallet takes part.
- **Float**: the Jackpot's own GRAM. It pays draws and node openings.
- **Keeper**: anyone who sends `submit_randomness`. It needs to send nothing else, apart from `refill` when the float is
  low.

**Units of the entry price.** `pot` is in hGRAM and `P` in GRAM, so the price uses `pot_v`, the value of the pot in
GRAM. With that, and with a pot above zero, `c` is about `t·P/F`: principal per free hGRAM.

**Rounding.** Every rounding favours the pool, so `F·TC ≥ P·TT` holds after each step while the rate does not
fall:

- **Booking:** `F` grows by `t`, and `P` by `c ≤ v ≤ t·TC/TT`.
- **Withdrawal:** `F` falls by at most `x·TT/TC`, and `P` by exactly `x`.
- **Draw:** `F` drops by `pot`, so to `need`.
- **Unstake:** `u ≤ pot`, taken before the prizes.
- **New quote at a higher rate:** only raises the left side.

`pot_v` rounds up, so the price rounds up. The `min` in `w` keeps its v1 purpose: if the rate falls, each
withdrawal takes its share of `F`.

## Context

- **Quote.** `provide_current_quote` answers with `take_current_quote` under the caller's `query_id`, with the
  remaining value, and has no stop check (`contract/contracts/treasury.fc:754`).
- **Deposits arrive as transfers.** The Jackpot's hGRAM wallet sends `transfer_notification` naming the
  sender (`contract/contracts/wallet.fc:85-95`) only if the transfer carried `forward_ton_amount`. A mint names
  the owner, here the Jackpot itself (`wallet.fc:129-135`). `deposit_coins` mints to the player
  (`treasury.fc:380`); the app does that before the Jackpot is involved.
- **Unstaking.** `unstake_tokens` moves `tokens` to `unstaking` (`wallet.fc:155`). The treasury then burns at
  once if it has the GRAM, makes a bill for a coming round if not, and rolls back if neither is possible
  (`treasury.fc:471-587`).
  - A burn ends in `withdrawal_notification(amount, coins)` to the owner, with the GRAM attached
    (`wallet.fc:211`).
  - A bill's burn carries the `query_id` of whatever started the round's burn, not the owner's
    (`treasury.fc:1242`, `contract/contracts/collection.fc:97`, `bill.fc:56`).
  - A rollback ends in `rollback_unstake`, which restores `tokens` and sends the owner a plain `gas_excess`
    (`wallet.fc:195`, `contract/contracts/parent.fc:98`). So does the wallet's own bounce handler
    (`wallet.fc:327`). v1 records that anyone can make that wallet send `gas_excess` with any `query_id`, so
    it proves nothing.
- **v1 tree.** `fold_held` folds at a later tag (`jackpot/contracts/node.fc:175`). `array_add` clamps at 0
  (`node.fc:111`). A range answers `check` (`jackpot/contracts/range.fc:104`). Fees are sized at 400 nano-GRAM
  per gas (`jackpot/contracts/imports/constants.fc:128-135`).
- **Chain values, read from config cells on 2026-10-31.** Config 18: basechain 0 per bit and 135 per cell since
  2026-04-29, masterchain 1000 per bit and 500,000 per cell, unchanged. Config 21: 66.67 nano-GRAM per gas,
  `freeze_due_limit` 0.1 GRAM, `delete_due_limit` 1 GRAM. Config 25: lump 66,667, 66 per bit, 6,666 per cell.

## Approach

### Contracts

1. **`jackpot`**: as v1, without the pending dict, the cutoff fields and `range_code`. New: `L`, `n_b`, `tag`,
   `U`, the schedule (`anchor`, `period`, the latest boundary, `next_period`, `switch_at`), the time of the last
   unstake, jetton `content`, and `wallet_code`. It is also the jGRAM master.
2. **`wallet`**: replaces `range`. Init data `(jackpot, owner)`; the first message adds `balance`, `b` (or none)
   and `pending`. Its code stays in full in its state.
3. **`node`**: see The tree. Init data `(jackpot, level, index)`. Its code stays in full in its state.

Wallet and node code are fixed, as in v1.

### The clock

The schedule never reads config. On every message except a plain top-up the tick runs first:

1. If `next_period` is set and `now ≥ switch_at`: `anchor = switch_at`, `period = next_period`, clear it.
2. Let `T_n` be the latest boundary at or before `now`. If it is not later than the stored one, stop.
3. Store `T_n`. Then by state:
   - **none:** take a new snapshot.
   - **resolving:** every open slot rolls over (`O −= p`, `carried += p`), the draw ends, and a new snapshot is taken.
   - **waiting or quoting:** a skip. State becomes waiting, `seed` is cleared, `R` moves to `T_n`'s round, and
     nothing else changes. The tree stays as it is and `tag` does not move.
4. **New snapshot:** `tag += 1`, `draw_L = L`. If `draw_L > 0`, state is waiting and `R` is `T_n`'s round;
   otherwise state stays none.

The tick has no loop, so one message handles any number of passed boundaries, and only the latest counts.

**The tick sends nothing.** It only changes storage. Burn batching waits for the end of the next draw, so the first
message after a boundary, perhaps a bare `withdrawn`, never pays for another step out of its own value.

**Why the snapshot is exact however late the tick runs.** Every change to `L` is made by a Jackpot handler, and
each handler ticks first. So no change can land between `T_n` and the tick, and `L` at the tick is `L` at `T_n`. A
late tick cannot help anyone even if `R` is already public, because the first message after `T_n` is the one that
records `draw_L`, ahead of its own effect. `D` remains only as a margin over a collator's freedom in block time.

**A period change** (`set_period`, governor) stores `next_period` and sets `switch_at` to the next boundary
of the current schedule. It changes nothing before that boundary: not the open draw's `R`, not its deadline.
Sending it again replaces the pending value. Before the first boundary, `anchor` is taken as the next boundary.

`phase`, the first `anchor`, is set at deploy so that a boundary falls about an hour after the time Hipo's
reward has landed in recent rounds. It has no safety role. A poor choice only makes the pot wait.

### Deposit

The player sends hGRAM to the Jackpot with `forward_ton_amount ≥ fee::deposit`, and optionally a forward
payload `deposit_options#_ min_c:uint128` inline. Each step below is one transaction.

1. **Notification at the Jackpot.** From its own hGRAM wallet only.
   - Sender = the Jackpot (a mint): ignored. The tokens stay outside `T`.
   - `amount = 0`: ignored. Sender off the basechain: held outside `T` for `return_tokens`, as v1.
   - Halted, or value below the fee: returned when the value pays for it, else held, as v1.
   - Otherwise `T += t`, `I += t`, and a record under a new id from `new_query_id()`, never the transfer's,
     `(prepare, owner, t, min_c, time)`. Later `query_id` in the flow means this id. It sends
     `prepare(query_id)` to `wallet_addr(owner)`, bounceable, with the wallet's StateInit, carrying the rest
     of the value.
   - A payload of any other shape means the default `min_c`. Parsing never throws.
2. **At the wallet.** If it is opening: throw, so the message bounces. Otherwise `pending += 1` and it answers
   `ready(query_id, b or none)` with the remaining value, bounceable, sent so that a failed send bounces the `prepare`.
3. **`ready` at the Jackpot.** Accepted only with a live prepare record and only from `wallet_addr(owner)` of
   that record. The record becomes `(booking, owner, t, min_c, b, time)`, and the Jackpot asks for a quote under
   the same `query_id`, with the remaining value. With no such record, because a `requote` ended it, or from any
   other sender, the Jackpot answers the sender with `release(query_id)` and the remaining value; a forged `ready`
   gets back only its own value. Every action here carries +16, so a failed action phase bounces the `ready` and the
   record stays for `requote`.
4. **The quote reply** books, in one step. `pot`, `pot_v` and `c` are read first, with `t` still in `I`, so a deposit
   never pays for its own tokens. Then `I −= t`, and:
   - **Refused** if halted, the rate has fallen, `v < min_deposit`, `c = 0`, `c < min_c`, or there is no position and
     `n_b = 16,777,216`. It pays `t` back (recorded payout) and sends the wallet `release(query_id)`.
   - **Booked otherwise.** `P += c`, `L += c`. With no position, `b = n_b` and `n_b += 1`. It sends
     `update(tag, b, +c)` down the tree, with the owner when `b` is new. It sends the wallet
     `credit(query_id, c, b)` with the rest of the value, and logs (owner, `b`, `c`, `t`).
5. **`credit` at the wallet.** `balance += c`, `pending −= 1`, stores `b`, tops its reserve up to
   `fee::wallet_storage`, and sends the rest to the owner.
6. **`release` at the wallet.** `pending −= 1`. If `balance = 0` and `pending = 0`, the wallet closes. A bounced `ready`
   counts as a `release`.

A `prepare` that bounces (wallet opening, or frozen) returns `t` to the owner. A `prepare` that never answers,
because a frozen wallet's debt ate its value, can be ended after 10 minutes by `requote`, which returns `t` and
deletes the record; a late `ready` then gets `release`, so `pending` cannot stay raised. `requote` on a booking record
works as in v1, with `fee::requote` sized for the step it pays for.

**Why the quote comes after the wallet.** Price, principal, tree update and the side of the boundary are then
all decided in one Jackpot transaction, so a deposit is in a draw if and only if its reply was handled before
the boundary, and it has paid exactly the price of that moment. A wallet never holds balance without a position.

**A `credit` that bounces** cannot happen, because `ready` has just shown the wallet alive and the value is
fixed. If it does, the booking is undone as v1 undoes a failed range setup: `P −= c`, `L −= c`,
`update(−c)`, `T −= w` at the stored quote, a log, and the tokens wait for `return_tokens`. The body carries
`c` and `b`, which fit in the 256 bits a bounce returns.

### Entry price

```
c = v                              if P = 0, or the draw state is waiting or quoting
c = floor(v·P / (P + pot_v))       otherwise
```

**Why it stops a sniper, at any size.** In exact arithmetic, booking moves `v − c` into the pot, so:

- `c = v·P/(P+Q)`, and it pays `v − c = v·Q/(P+Q)`.
- After booking, `P' = P·(P+Q+v)/(P+Q)` and `Q' = Q·(P+Q+v)/(P+Q)`, so `Q'/P' = Q/P`. Pot per principal is
  unchanged.
- Its share of the tickets is `c/P' = v/(P+Q+v)`, so its expected prize is `Q'·v/(P+Q+v) = v·Q/(P+Q)`: what it
  paid, whatever `v` and `Q` are.

The burn takes `b_share` of that payment and gas takes more, so its expected gain is below zero. Checked in
exact fractions at `v` from 10 to 10^8 GRAM and `Q/P` from 0.03% to 1000%: expected prize minus price is 0
without the burn, and `−β·(v − c)` with it, when `carried` is 0.

**A deposit in an open draw.**

- **Waiting or quoting:** the pot is not yet set aside, and all of it belongs to the snapshot. So `c = v`. `F`
  grows by `t` and `need` by about `t`, so the pot, and so the draw, is unchanged to within rounding.
- **Resolving:** the pot is already in `O`, and what is in `F` is new. The normal formula charges for exactly
  that, which is right, because this deposit joins the next draw.
- **Skip:** `c = v` would be a free share of the rolled-over pot if the tickets entered the next draw. They do
  not: a skip takes no snapshot, so the same tickets draw for the rolled-over pot, and tickets booked since then
  wait until that draw has ended. This is why a skip does not fold. This needs the searches to finish before the
  boundary, which the cut-off `S` gives: a draw that reaches resolving has at least 1,800 s left, or 900 s after a
  resent quote, which is about 90 hops of retries.

The deposit's own yield during those minutes goes to the running draw's pot. In a long outage that is real: a
deposit made in it earns no chance and no yield until the draw ends.

**Timing around Hipo's reward.** The rate rises by `r` when a round's reward lands. Results below were computed in
exact fractions.

| Move | Expected result | Compared with |
|---|---|---|
| Deposit `v` just before the reward | pays the price of today's pot, whose prize returns it; then `r·v` of expected prize from the step, less `β` | holding that hGRAM outside: `r·v`. Not better |
| Stay in over the reward | `(1−β)·r·x`, plus the share of the old pot | |
| Leave just before, stay out | `r·x` for certain, and gives up its share of the old pot to the others | it is no longer playing, and pays no burn |
| Leave just before, re-enter just after | from an empty pot: `r·x` outside, then a price of about `r·x` at the new pot, whose prize is worth that less `β`: `(1−β)·r·x` less two chains of gas | equal to staying, less gas; with a pot already there it also gives up that share |

Why the first row holds: the reward is `r·(P+Q+v)`, and the deposit's ticket share is `v/(P+Q+v)`, so its
expected part is `r·v`, the yield of its own hGRAM. The Jackpot turns yield into a lottery on that yield and
adds nothing to it. So a sniper who owns hGRAM anyway gains nothing from the timing. A player can cut their
variance by leaving and re-entering at a cost in gas, and takes nothing from the others.

Rows 3 and 4 assume the treasury answered the withdrawal before the reward. If the reward lands before the quote is
asked, the withdrawal is paid at the new rate, so the player leaves with `x` and no `r·x`; re-entry then gains about
`−β·price`, never positive.

**Other limits of the price.**

- **`P = 0`:** `c = v`, so the first depositor after everyone has left owns what was left in the pot. No player
  is harmed, as nobody is in.
- **A very large price.** With few players and a large pot, a deposit could book far less than it brought,
  although fair on average. `min_c` is what stops that. The default refuses a price above 1%, and the app sends
  an explicit `min_c`, which also covers a reward landing between the signature and the booking.
- **A prize that rolls over during resolving** returns to `F` after some deposits were priced without it, and the
  next snapshot takes in tickets booked at `c = v` while the draw waited, so those deposits share it for free. With
  the cut-off `S` this needs 16 misses in a row or a stuck node. A colluding whale cannot profit: it must empty a
  share `g` of the snapshot, giving up `g·pot`, to make a slot roll with chance `g^16 < g`, even if it books again at
  `c = v`. An outsider who sees from the seed that all 16 attempts will miss can book before the last miss and share
  that slot free. That needs others to have emptied most of the snapshot, the chance per slot is `g^16`, and it is
  accepted.
- **A draw that ends with `pot < m`** leaves that pot in `F`, and deposits booked at `c = v` share it: at most `m`,
  and repeatedly while a round's yield is below `m` (today, `P` under about 3,200 GRAM). A pot within rounding of `m`
  also lets someone who has seen the seed flip the draw to "nothing paid" with a deposit's rounding, if they beat the
  keeper; the prize at stake is at most `m`. Both are accepted.
- **`P` lags `L` by one hop.** A withdrawal stays in `P` until its quote reply, so a booking handled in that gap is
  priced a little low. It cannot be forced, and a whale that arranges it loses money. Accepted.

### The tree

A node holds three arrays of 64 sums, in v1's layout: `committed`, `added`, `removed`. Empty arrays share their
cells. A level-1 node also holds `owners`: 64 256-bit address hashes (owners are basechain), 3 to a cell, in a
small tree of refs.

- **`update(tag, b, delta, owner?)`** starts at the Jackpot and goes down as in v1. Each node adds `delta` to
  `added` or `−delta` to `removed` for the child `ch` holding `b`. It never touches `committed`. There is no deferred
  flag: every change is held until the next snapshot, whether a draw is open or not.
- **Fold.** On any message with a tag later than the node's own: `committed[ch] += added[ch] − removed[ch]`, both
  arrays are cleared, and the node takes the tag. A node untouched for several tags folds once.
- **`descend`** reads `committed` only, as in v1. A level-1 node then answers itself: if the offset inside child
  `ch` is below `committed[ch] − removed[ch]`, it sends the Jackpot `hit(tag, i, j, k, b, owner)`; otherwise
  `miss(tag, i, j, k, b)`. No wallet is asked.
- **A level-1 node ends an update** by sending the remaining value to `owners[ch]`.
- **Opening a node.** An update that carries an owner opens the child when `b` is its first. The first node of each
  level is deployed from the float, later ones from each new position's node share, as in v1. An ordinary
  top-up at such a `b` opens nothing.

**Why nothing is applied directly after a draw ends.** A direct negative update in the rest of the period could
reach `committed` before the `added` it cancels, and `array_add`'s clamp at 0 would lose it for good. Holding
everything removes that case. The node invariant is plain: `committed` is the snapshot, and
`committed + added − removed` is the truth.

**Why every search sees the snapshot.** Updates and searches start at the Jackpot and share each parent-child
pair, which delivers in order. A search with tag `q` finds every node on its path folded up to every update sent
before snapshot `q`, and no later update in `committed`. The answer now comes from the same ordered line as the
update, so unlike v1 no reply races a wallet.

**Why positions cannot be moved after the seed.** `committed` cannot change until the next snapshot. A deposit
during a draw lands in `added`. A withdrawal lands in `removed`, which can only turn the owner's own hit into a
miss. A wallet that withdraws and then deposits again stays a miss, because `removed` does not fall.

**A withdrawal counts against the snapshot's tickets first.** A player with 100 in the snapshot who deposits 50
and withdraws 30 in the same period has 70 live for that draw. It can only cost them, and tracking it finer is
not worth a field.

### Withdrawal

1. The owner sends `withdraw(x)` to the wallet (`x = 0` means all, and needs a balance above 0). It checks the owner,
   `fee::withdraw`, that `x ≤ balance`, and `x = balance` or `x ≥ 1 GRAM`. `balance −= x`, and it sends
   `withdrawn(query_id, x, last, b, owner)` bounceable, with `last` true when the balance is now 0. When `last`,
   `pending += 1` as well.
2. The Jackpot ticks and checks `src = wallet_addr(owner)`. It then never throws: not while halted, not on a
   fallen rate, not in a draw. Every action here carries +16, so a failed action phase bounces `withdrawn` and `x` is
   not lost. `L −= x`, `update(tag, b, −x)`, a quote record `(withdrawal, owner, b, x)`, and if `last`,
   `close(query_id)` to the wallet. The rest of the value rides on the quote.
   The record and the quote use a new id from `new_query_id()`. The wallet's `query_id` is never a key of a record,
   because a player could then overwrite any open record.
3. On the reply: `w` as defined, `P −= x`, `T −= w`, a recorded payout. Until then `x` stays in `P`.

If `withdrawn` bounces, for instance if the Jackpot could not pay its sends, the wallet restores `x` and sends the
value to the owner. A bounced `withdrawn` with `last` also lowers `pending` by 1.

**Closing.** On `close` from the Jackpot the wallet lowers `pending` by 1, then checks `balance = 0` and `pending = 0`. If so it sends all its
GRAM to the owner in the message that deletes the account (modes 128 + 32). If not, it does nothing. The raised
`pending` stops a `release` from closing a wallet while a full `withdrawn` is in flight. The wallet
does not close at `withdraw`, because if it were gone a bounce of `withdrawn` would have nowhere to restore to.

**A frozen wallet.** A wallet that froze with changed data cannot be revived from its original StateInit. With the
rule above, a wallet stays at balance 0 only while a deposit is in flight, so a frozen wallet with balance 0 and
changed data is not a state the design reaches. A closed wallet is an empty address, and `prepare`'s StateInit
deploys it again with a new position. Its old position is dead: all its tickets have left, and its
`owners` entry is never read again.

A wallet frozen with a balance above 0 refuses `prepare`, so a deposit returns its `t`. The owner, or anyone, can
revive it by sending its exact last state and paying its debt (`scripts/reviveWallet.ts`), and then all works.
Prizes do not need the wallet, so a frozen wallet can still win.

**Positions are never reused.** A closed wallet that comes back takes a new one. At 16,777,216 positions, new
wallets are refused and their `t` returned, while existing wallets, withdrawals and draws go on. Exhausting the
tree on purpose costs each position's gas and node share, about 0.0125 GRAM at today's prices (an estimate; the reserve
comes back on close), so about 210,000 GRAM. Nothing is lost if it happens, and the governor could start a new tree by
upgrade.

**Races.**

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Two deposits, wallet has `b` | both `ready(b)`; both book; `pending` falls to 0 |
| Two deposits, new wallet | the first makes it opening; the second `prepare` bounces and its `t` returns. Nobody else can cause this, since only the owner's transfer names the owner |
| Full withdrawal while a deposit is in flight | the withdrawal adds 1 to `pending`, so `close` only lowers it; `credit` arrives on the same `b`, or `release` closes the wallet |
| `prepare` arrives after a close | a fresh deployment, a new position. The old `withdrawn` is keyed by the old `b`, so each update goes where it belongs |
| Withdrawal in flight when a search passes | the Jackpot has not seen it, so the ticket counts and can hit. It was in the snapshot and the principal is the owner's anyway, so this gains nothing |

### Draw

1. **`submit_randomness(signature, witness)`** comes from anyone, as an internal message with at least
   `fee::submit`. In order, cheapest first: tick; state is waiting; `now < next boundary − S`; not halted; rate-fell
   flag down; `G` covers `fee::draw_budget`; then the v1 drand check for `R`. A failed check throws, so junk pays for
   itself. On success: store `seed`, state quoting, and ask for a quote under a record `(draw, submitter, T_n)`. A second
   submitter fails on the state check at small cost.
2. **The quote reply fixes the draw.**
   - Every quote reply, of any kind, checks for a fall first, as v1 does, and raises the flag. The cases below come
     after that.
   - Record no longer current: a record is current only if the state is quoting and its `T_n` is the stored boundary.
     If not, the rest of the value goes to the submitter, with no reward, and the state is left as it is.
   - Reply after `next boundary − S/2`, which only a lost quote resent by `requote` can cause: state back to waiting,
     `seed` cleared, the rest goes to the submitter, with no reward. A draw must not reach resolving that late.
   - The rate fell: the flag rises, state returns to waiting, `seed` is cleared, the rest goes to the submitter.
   - `pot < m`: the draw ends with nothing paid, and the pot stays in `F`.
   - Otherwise: compute `u`, then `pot'`, `burn` and the prizes as in the table. `T −= u`, `U += u`, send the
     unstake if `u > 0`; `O += pot'`, `burn_owed += burn`, `carried = 0`; state resolving; send three
     `descend(tag, i, 0, k(i,0))` from the float.
   - In the last two cases the submitter receives the rest of the value plus `r_s`, once per draw. It is paid
     even when `pot < m`, because the tree can only move on when a draw ends.
3. **Searches.** On `hit` from `node(1, b >> 6)` for the current tag, slot and attempt, with `k` matching: the slot
   closes and the prize is paid to `owner` as a recorded payout, or rolled over if the flag is up. On `miss` or
   `lost`, the next attempt goes out from the float, up to `A`. After `A` the prize rolls over.
4. **End.** When all slots are closed the state is none. Burn batching runs as in v1, its gas from the float. The
   tree moves at the next boundary.

`R` is known in advance and `seed` is not. Anyone may submit from when drand publishes `R` until `S` before the next
boundary. Without the cut-off, a submit in the last seconds would lose its searches to the boundary, and the whole pot
would roll into a snapshot holding tickets booked at `c = v`, free of price and of burn.

**Boundary while a draw is running, in one place.** Only one draw is ever open.

- **Searches in flight:** the boundary ends the draw and open slots roll over. Later `hit` and `miss` messages carry
  an old tag and are ignored, and their value stays in the float. A search already sent cannot be overtaken, so it
  still reads the old snapshot. With the cut-off `S`, only a stuck node gets here.
- **A stuck slot** (a node with no GRAM, say) rolls over at the boundary. No `expire` message exists.
- **`quoting` at a boundary:** a skip, and the late reply refunds the submitter, because its record's `T_n` no longer
  matches.
- **Withdrawals** are never involved: a stuck draw blocks nothing but its own prizes, and drand down only delays them.

**The griefing bound, no surcharge.** A withdrawal in a draw leaves empty places that cost retries, paid from yield.

- **Hard bound:** 3 slots × 16 attempts = 48 searches per draw, all from the float. The worst real attempt is about
  290,000 gas, when every hop folds; after the first fold per node per tag it is about 68,000. Only the first 3
  attempts or so can fold every node, so the bound is about 3 × 290,000 + 45 × 68,000 = 3.9 million gas: about
  0.26 GRAM at 66.67 nano-GRAM per gas and about 1.6 GRAM at 400. These are estimates until measured.
  `fee::draw_budget` is 4.66 GRAM, above both.
- **Expected cost:** for a share `g` of tickets gone, a slot needs `1 + g + … + g^15` attempts, so extra attempts per
  draw are at most `45·g`.
- **What it costs the griefer:** to come near the bound they need most of the snapshot's tickets, and leaving gives
  up that share of the pot. They also pay a withdrawal. Coming back while the draw waits costs no price, so giving up
  `g·pot` is what bounds this.

So the griefer gives up about `g·pot` to cost the others at most about 0.26 GRAM a draw at today's prices. It ends with
the period, leaves nothing in later draws, and the same empty places make the honest players more likely
to win. Hence no surcharge.

**Whales.** After a fold, withdrawn tickets have no place in the tree, so nothing carries over to later draws. Each
rollover is plain, as in v1. Attempts are independent, so a player's chance depends on tickets, not on how they are
spread over addresses.

### Gas

**User chains** (deposit, withdrawal, claim). The entry minimums stay sized at 400 nano-GRAM per gas, worst case, so a
price rise cannot strand a chain. What is not spent comes back:

- Each handler passes on what it received less its gas, using a reserve of the Jackpot's balance before the
  message and mode 128, so a user chain never moves the float. Fixed branches, such as the tree's `update`, are
  taken out first. A reply short of value aborts and its record stays for `requote`, which also covers a booking at a
  new node with the float below what opening it costs.
- Each branch ends at the owner: the update's at the level-1 node, a booking's at the wallet, a refused booking's
  and a withdrawal's at `gas_excess`.
- `return_excess` stays the Jackpot, because confirmation needs it. A `gas_excess` that confirms a user-paid
  payout forwards its remaining value to the recipient (mode 64, never the float). Prize and burn confirmations stay
  in the float. A forged confirmation forwards only the forger's own value, but see the accepted limits under Risks.

Kept by a deposit: the real gas, the wallet reserve (returned on close), and, for a new position, the node share.

`fee::deposit`, `fee::withdraw`, `fee::submit`, `fee::unstake`, `fee::refill` and `fee::draw_budget` are constants
measured by tests. `fee::draw_budget` is the most one draw can spend: 48 searches, 3 payouts, one burn, `r_s` and
`fee::unstake`, plus the value that three searches carry while in flight. `G_lo` is above it, because otherwise a draw
could never see `G` low enough to refill.

**Draws** come from the float: submit reimbursement, searches, prize payouts, burn gas. A normal draw should cost
about 0.08 GRAM today (an estimate, to be measured), plus 0.5 GRAM for a burn.

**Refilling the float.** At a draw with `G < G_lo`, if `U = 0` or the last unstake was over 7 days ago, the Jackpot
sends its hGRAM wallet `unstake_tokens(u)` in mode auto, with `fee::unstake` GRAM from the float. `u` is capped at a
fifth of the pot, so a small pot is not eaten. `u` leaves `T` at once, so `T` never exceeds what the wallet holds. The 7
days only guard against a stale `U`.

- **`refill`,** from anyone, with at least `fee::refill`: accepted in state none or waiting when
  `G < fee::draw_budget` and `u` would be allowed. It asks for a quote under a record `(refill, sender)`. The reply
  raises the flag if the rate fell, computes `u` as at a draw, with `u = 0` while the flag is up or unless
  `G < fee::draw_budget` and the state is none or waiting, sends the unstake on the sender's value, and returns the
  rest. A draw needs `G ≥ fee::draw_budget`, so once `G` is below that no draw can refill, and a period with no draw
  would stay stuck without this message. In normal running only the draw refills, at most once per draw and after the
  `pot < m` test, so nobody can shrink a pot once the seed is public.
- **`withdrawal_notification`,** from the Jackpot's hGRAM wallet only: `U = max(U − amount, 0)`. The GRAM stays in
  the float. It is matched by sender, never by `query_id`, because a bill's burn carries someone else's. It works the
  same for an instant burn and for a deferred one rounds later. This handler never throws.
- **A bounce of `unstake_tokens`** from that wallet: the wallet refused it, `T += u`, `U −= u`.
- **Rollback.** The treasury's rollback restores the tokens in the wallet, but its only signal is a `gas_excess`
  that anyone can forge, so the Jackpot ignores it. The tokens sit outside `T`, which is safe. The governor checks
  the wallet and sends `settle_unstake(amount)`: `U −= amount`, `T += amount`. The 7-day rule stops a stale `U` from
  blocking later refills.

If `G` cannot cover `fee::draw_budget`, `submit_randomness` throws, and draws skip until someone sends `refill` or tops
the float up. Anyone may. A small pot refills slowly, as `u` is at most a fifth of it. If gas prices rise, the governor
raises `m`.

### Rules kept from v1

  and `descend` only from its parent; a wallet hears `prepare`, `credit`, `release`, `close` only from the Jackpot, and

- **Basechain only** for owners.
- **Records used once**, deleted by the first reply; `requote` after 10 minutes moves a record to a fresh `query_id`.
- **Every entry message pays its whole chain.** The minimum is checked before any change. `submit_randomness` pays

- **Reply handlers never throw:** `take_current_quote`, notifications, `ready`, `hit`, `miss`, `lost`, bounces,

- **Payout records** and all the `gas_excess` facts: success only from the recipient's hGRAM wallet; from the Jackpot's

- **Falling rate:** as v1. While the flag is up, bookings return their tokens, submits are refused (so draws skip),

- **Halted:** deposits return their tokens and submits are refused. A running draw completes and withdrawals go on.
- **Burn batching** and `carried`: as v1. Skipped draws add nothing to `carried`, as their yield was never burned.
- **Upgrades:** `upgrade_code` with `migrate` through `set_c3`, in one transaction. `migrate` keeps the draw state and

- **Deploy checks and setters:** `b_share ≤ 2000`, `min_deposit ≥ 1`, `3600 ≤ period ≤ 2,592,000`.
- **Governor:** as v1 without `resolve_pending`, plus `set_period`, `set_content`, `settle_unstake`. It sets period, burn

### Rejected alternatives

- **A skip that folds the held changes:** deposits booked at `c = v` while the draw waited would share the
  rolled-over pot free, and anyone could take that during an outage.
- **A full entry price while waiting, refunded later:** a refund needs a message to every such wallet.
- **Holding the deposit in a pending record until the pot is fixed:** the pending dict again, plus a driver for it.
- **Fixing the pot at the tick:** it needs a quote then, and it does not solve the skip.
- **Liveness kept in the wallet, as a range did:** `b → wallet` has no derivation, a frozen wallet would stall a
  slot, and the answer would race the tree.
- **A map from owner to `b` in the Jackpot:** account size limit.
- **A wallet that stays with balance 0 and keeps `b`:** the frozen-with-changed-data problem.
- **Resetting a wallet to its init data at balance 0:** works, but it keeps paying rent for nothing and still loses `b`.
- **A free list of positions:** a position freed while a deposit is in flight could go to two owners, for a capacity
  that is not near.
- **Crediting the wallet before the quote or before the tree update:** a wallet could hold balance with no position,
  and a boundary could fall between the price and the tickets.
- **`withdrawn` that destroys the wallet at once:** a bounce would lose the player's claim.
- **Treating `gas_excess` as an unstake rollback:** anyone can forge it, and `T` would overcount.
- **Applying updates directly once a draw ends:** the clamp case above.
- **A signed single `held` array:** withdraw-then-deposit would restore a withdrawn ticket.
- **Wallet or node code as a library cell:** library cells are being specialized for other uses, the masterchain rent
  is about 7.5 and 11.9 GRAM a year, and losing a library would stop every wallet.

## Acceptance criteria

**Carried over from v1, rewritten for v2's messages.** Solvency and wallet-≥-`T` property test
(`Acceptance`, `Property`); full exit returns principal less 1 nano-hGRAM per withdrawal; falling rate; lost quote
and `requote`, repeated and late; payout bounce, claim, second-hop failure, forged `gas_excess`; two claims; failed
burn; burn only on `pot' − carried`; masterchain sender; one owner wins two slots; drand tests (`Drand.spec.ts`, whole
file); halted; governor setters and deploy checks; upgrades and `UpgradeDryRun.spec.ts`; `HipoHarness.spec.ts`; gas pins
per handler with a worst-case fold (`Gas.spec.ts`); `return_tokens`, `set_hipo_wallet`; forged tree messages.

**Dropped with the code they test.** Bill mints, `resolve_pending`, range setup, surcharge, `expire`, `locate`, the
cutoff tests (`AC 4`, `AC 7b`, `AC 20`), and the keeper-driven parts of `AC 7`, `AC 16`, `AC 18`.

**New.**

- [ ] **Property.** A sandbox run of random deposits, withdrawals, reward steps, submits, skips and boundaries, at least
      10,000 sequences. After every transaction `F·TC ≥ P·TT`, `T = I + O + F`, the real hGRAM wallet ≥ `T`. Once settled,
      every node's `committed + added − removed` equals the balances below it and `L` equals their sum.
- [ ] **Winners recomputed.** In the same run each winner equals the owner of the `k(i,j)`-th ticket of the snapshot,
      recomputed off chain from logs alone, and each miss is a ticket withdrawn since the snapshot.
- [x] **Sniper, sandbox and exact.** For `v` of 0.01, 1, 100 and 10,000 times `P`, depositing right before a boundary
      with a full pot: over at least 1,000 seeds per size, mean prize minus `v − c` is ≤ 0 within sampling error, and an
      exact-fraction script shows `Q'/P' = Q/P` and expected gain = `−β·(v−c)`. A test confirms the on-chain `c` to
      the nano against the formula.
- [x] **Timing.** A test for each row of the timing table, with the reward landing between two messages, shows the
      expected value given there, within sampling error, including a quote reply on each side of the step.
- [x] **Open draw.** A deposit in waiting or quoting books `c = v` and leaves the pot, to within 2 nano-hGRAM, and no
      winner has it. A deposit in resolving pays the normal price and joins the next draw.
- [x] **Skip does not let a deposit share the pot.** After a skip, a deposit booked while waiting wins nothing in the
      draw that pays the rolled-over pot, over many seeds, and joins the next one.
- [x] **`min_c`.** A booking with `c < min_c` returns `t` whole and leaves `P` and `L` unchanged. With no payload, or a
      malformed one, a price above 1% is refused and no handler throws.
- [x] **Price read before booking.** At pot 0, `c = v` to the nano for `v` from 0.01 to 10,000 times `P`.
- [x] **The tick sends nothing.** With a slot open at a boundary and `burn_owed` over the batch, the first message after
      the boundary is a `withdrawn` carrying exactly `fee::withdraw`. It pays, and the burn goes at the end of the next
      draw.
- [x] **A failed action loses nothing.** A forced action-phase failure in `withdrawn` bounces and the wallet restores
      `x`. One in `ready` bounces, the wallet's `pending` falls, and `requote` returns `t`.
- [x] **Submit cut-off.** With a valid signature, a submit at `next boundary − S` throws with no state change, and one
      a second earlier succeeds. With a draw that runs from the last allowed second, all searches finish before the
      boundary.
- [x] **Stale draw reply.** A draw reply handled after a boundary refunds the submitter, pays no reward, leaves state
      and `seed` as the tick set them, never searches with a cleared seed, and does not touch a newer submit. A reply
      after `next boundary − S/2` is refunded the same way, and the state returns to waiting with `seed` cleared.
- [x] **Stale `ready`.** `requote` on a prepare record with `ready` still in flight returns `t` once and brings
      `pending` back to 0. A new wallet is not left opening, and a wallet with a position can close.
- [x] **Zero withdrawal.** `withdraw(0)` at balance 0 throws in the wallet.
- [x] **Refill.** A stranger's `refill` restores a float below `fee::draw_budget` while a draw waits. Over 100 periods
      at `P` = 5,000 GRAM, `G` stays above `fee::draw_budget`. `G` excludes the message's value at the submit gate and
      in `u`. `G_lo > fee::draw_budget`. `refill` throws at `G ≥ fee::draw_budget`, and its `u` is 0 while the flag is
      up.
- [x] **Post-seed withdrawal.** A player withdraws after `R` is public and before the search. Every slot's winner is the
      same as without it, unless the winner's ticket was the one withdrawn, which then misses and moves to `k(i,j+1)`. A
      withdrawal followed by a deposit stays a miss.
- [x] **Held both ways.** With deposits and withdrawals on the same `b` in one period, in every order, `committed` does
      not change until the next tag, then equals the true sum. A negative after the draw ends is not lost.
- [x] **Whale.** A whale holding 10,000 times the honest total in the snapshot leaves during the draw. That draw costs
      at most 48 searches, the whale wins nothing, and what no one wins rolls over. The next draw pays the rolled pot, the
      honest players win at least 90% of it, and every later draw logs 3 hits and 0 misses. A whale that books and leaves
      before the boundary, or after it without being in the snapshot, changes nothing in the draw.
- [x] **Griefing bound.** The most float one draw can spend is pinned at or below `fee::draw_budget`, in the worst
      case of 48 searches, 3 payouts and a burn.
- [x] **Clock.** A deposit booked one second before a boundary can win that draw; one booked one second after cannot.
      With no message for 5 periods, the first message records `draw_L` as at the latest boundary, once, ahead
      of its own effect, whatever its kind; `R` matches the off-chain rule; gas of the tick does not grow with the gap.
- [x] **Period change.** `set_period` during an open draw leaves its `R` and deadline alone; the new period starts at the
      next boundary; values outside the bounds are refused, by the setter and at deploy.
- [x] **Hands-free.** N = 50 periods run with `submit_randomness` as the only message besides the treasury's replies and
      reward steps: 3 prizes each, nothing else sent by anyone.
- [x] **Outage.** drand silent across two boundaries, with deposits and withdrawals meanwhile: all pay; on return one
      draw pays all the yield, `carried` is unchanged, burn is taken once; the deposits join the next draw; no principal
      or pot is lost; a round from before the latest boundary is refused.
- [x] **Boundary mid-search.** A `hit` delivered after the boundary pays nothing and its prize is in `carried`; a slot
      whose node never answers rolls over at the boundary; two draws are never open.
- [x] **Submit.** Junk throws and changes nothing; a second submitter fails at the state check; the first ends the draw
      ahead by `r_s` less its gas; `r_s` is paid once per draw; a submit with gas under its need fails with no state
      change.
- [x] **Wallet one-per-player.** Two deposits merge into one balance and one position; one withdrawal covers all.
      `send_tokens` throws with the balance unchanged. `get_wallet_data`, `get_jetton_data` and `get_wallet_address`
      answer as TEP-74 says, and once settled total supply equals the sum of balances.
- [x] **Message flow.** Every row of the Races table runs in the sandbox, in both delivery orders where order can vary,
      and ends with tree, wallet and `P` agreeing. A wallet never has balance > 0 with no position.
- [x] **Closed and frozen wallets.**
  - A wallet fully withdrawn is deleted, and its GRAM reaches the owner.
  - It can deposit again, gets a new position, and the old position is never picked.
  - A sandbox wallet frozen with balance above 0 returns a deposit's `t` whole, can be revived with its exact state, and
    then withdraws.
  - A wallet frozen at balance 0 with `pending` stale, a state made by hand since the design does not reach it,
    returns `t` through the bounce or through `requote`, and is not a loss of funds.
- [x] **No way to stop withdrawals.** A withdrawal pays while halted, with the flag up, in every draw state, with `G = 0`
      float, with drand silent, and with a node that has no GRAM.
- [x] **Gas returned.** For a deposit and a withdrawal at the minimum and at 1 GRAM over it, GRAM sent minus GRAM returned
      equals the sum of the chain's real fees, plus the reserve and node share for a deposit, plus at most 0.002 GRAM. The
      Jackpot's balance does not fall in any user chain.
- [x] **Float.** Over 50 periods with a real pot and Hipo's real unstake, `G` stays above `fee::draw_budget`. Separate
      tests: an instant burn, a deferred bill whose notification has a foreign `query_id`, a rollback that stays outside
      `T` until `settle_unstake`, a bounce, and a forged `gas_excess` with the unstake's `query_id` that changes nothing.
- [x] **Mint ignored.** A mint to the Jackpot leaves `T`, `I` and `P` unchanged, and `return_tokens` can return it.
- [x] Two money audits (one to prove it correct, one to make it lose money) and a code review pass on the code.

**Verification, 2026-11-01.** The suite passes: 195 tests in 15 suites at short sizes, and hands-free 50, refill 100, float 50
periods and sniper 1,000 seeds per size at full size. Property and Winners recomputed stay open: 1,717 of 10,000 sequences
ran, with no failure. Most tests use a drand stub; the bill, rollback and bounce tests start from edited state.

## Risks & rollback

- **No honest keeper.** With every keeper down, a player could submit only in rounds they win, since a skip costs them
  nothing. Pot accumulates for whoever does. Two keepers and an alarm on any skip are the answer.
- **A low float acts like an absent keeper.** While `G < fee::draw_budget` keepers cannot submit, and a player who has
  seen the seed can top up and submit only if they win. Alarm on `G < G_lo`, and keepers send `refill` on their own.
- **drand halts or retires.** Draws skip; the rest works. Detect: a skip. The governor can upgrade to another source.
- **Gaps across a long outage.** The snapshot stays, so withdrawn tickets stay as misses until the draw ends. It costs
  retries within the bound, and a prize may roll over.
- **Hipo changes unstake, its notifications, the quote, or migrates wallets.** The treasury's upgrade checklist must
  include the Jackpot.
- **A bill or rollback leaves `U` stale.** Detect: `U > 0` past two rounds. Fix: `settle_unstake`, after checking.
- **A wallet runs out of storage.** It freezes after a 0.1 GRAM debt, and past a 1 GRAM debt the account is deleted, with
  its record of the balance: decades away at today's prices, sooner if prices rise a lot. Top-ups are open to anyone,
  and monitoring watches wallets with balances.
- **A node runs out of storage.** Updates to it are lost and sums go wrong. Detect: `lost` logs and a sum mismatch against
  `L`. Node balances are monitored and any node can be topped up.
- **A bug in a node or a wallet.** Their code is fixed. The Jackpot can be upgraded and halted; a new tree is possible.
- **The tree fills.** Detect: `n_b` above 90%.
- **A stranger can confirm a payout early (accepted 2026-11-01).** Anyone can send the recipient 1 nano-hGRAM with the
  payout's `query_id` and `return_excess` set to the Jackpot. The recipient's hGRAM wallet then sends the `gas_excess`,
  the record closes, and the real confirmation finds nothing, so the excess stays in the float: 0.84 GRAM in a test sent
  with 1 GRAM. The attacker gains nothing and pays gas. If that first hop then bounces, the tokens wait outside `T` for
  `return_tokens`. A real fix needs another way of confirming payouts.
- **`claim` keeps a little (accepted 2026-11-01).** A claim's payouts are not user-paid, so their confirmation stays in
  the float: about 0.05 GRAM per payout.
- **Message order.** Exactness rests on TON's per-pair delivery order, which the sandbox cannot show.
- **The governor key:** as v1.

Rollback: nothing is deployed yet. Before funds are in, redeploy. After, halt and let players withdraw.

## Open questions

None. Both were decided on 2026-10-31:

1. **Library code:** none. Wallet and node code stay in full in each account's state, and no rent or librarian is
   involved.
2. **Deposits during a long outage:** they stay open, at `c = v`, with no tickets and no yield until the draw ends. The
   app must show this.
